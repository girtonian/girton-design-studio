## Your Four Case Studies

### 01 — Tessello
**Type:** Creative Technology / Generative Design Tool
**When:** Figma Makeathon 2026
**The hook:** You built a parametric pattern generator with 18 pattern types — hex, Penrose, Voronoi — inside Figma, at a Figma-hosted competition. This is design-meets-mathematics-meets-toolmaking. It's rare. It belongs first.

**Draft framing:**
> "A parametric pattern generator built in Figma over a weekend. 18 pattern types — hex, Penrose, Voronoi — with live parameter controls. The question wasn't 'can I build this' but 'what does it mean when designers build their own tools?'"

**What to show:** Screenshots of the pattern outputs, the Figma UI, the parameter controls. If there's a process video, even better.

**What to write up:**
- Why parametric / generative design matters
- The constraints of Figma as a build environment (and how you worked with them)
- What pattern types you chose and why (Penrose and Voronoi are non-trivial mathematical choices)
- What you'd build next

### 02 — Project Kyzen
**Type:** Game UX / Product Design
**When:** Jan 2022 – Sep 2024 (Impact Theory)
**The hook:** Nearly 3 years designing UX for a third-person battle arena with avatar customization — for Tom Bilyeu's company. This is high-complexity UX (game systems are brutal to design well), for a known media entrepreneur, over a sustained period.

**Draft framing:**
> "Three years designing the UX for a third-person battle arena. Avatar customization, combat flow, progression systems. Game design teaches you that every screen is a contract — break it once and you lose the player forever."

**What to show:** Any UI screens, character/avatar systems, HUD design, menus, onboarding flows.

**What to write up:**
- The specific UX problems unique to a battle arena (speed, legibility under stress, customization depth vs. discoverability)
- What you learned designing for games that transferred to other work
- Your role relative to the team
- Any pivots or major design decisions

## About Page — Draft

### Short version (for sidebar / cards):

> Jonathan Girton is a Design Futurist based in Chicago. He's spent over a decade working at the edges of design — game UX, generative tools, Web3, and edtech — following the frontier wherever it moves.

### Long version (for the About page proper):

> I've always been interested in what design can do when it stops looking backward.
>
> I studied graphic design at Harrington College in Chicago, then spent years working in spaces that hadn't fully figured out what design even meant yet — game UX, the creator economy, Web3. The work was harder, more ambiguous, more interesting. I kept going toward the edges.
>
> Somewhere in the middle, I got certified in additive manufacturing at MIT. Not because I planned to become an engineer, but because understanding how physical things are made changes how you think about all systems design. Constraints reveal structure.
>
> I'm currently founding Curmunchkins — an edtech platform designed from the ground up for neurodivergent kids. It's the most challenging design problem I've worked on, because the users are kids who've been failed by "one-size-fits-all" for their entire lives. Getting it right matters.
>
> In parallel, I build tools. Tessello — a parametric pattern generator — came out of a Figma Makeathon and reflects a simple belief: designers who make their own tools see differently.

### Beliefs (optional pullout):

- Design at the frontier is messier but more honest than design for proven markets
- Constraints are the most underrated design tool
- Sensory-first isn't a niche — it's better design for everyone
- A designer who can build is a different kind of designer
