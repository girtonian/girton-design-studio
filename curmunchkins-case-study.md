# Curmunchkins — Case Study
### Edtech Product / Founder / Sensory-First Design · Ongoing (Current)

---

## The Frame

> "Most products are retrofitted for accessibility. Curmunchkins is designed from the ground up for the kids who need it most."

---

## The Bet

Curmunchkins is not a client project. It's a bet.

There's a well-documented gap in educational technology: the products that exist were designed for neurotypical users and then adjusted for everyone else. The adjustment is always visible. It's a layer of accommodation over a foundation that wasn't built to accommodate.

The kids who fall through this gap aren't edge cases. ADHD. Autism. Dyslexia. Dyspraxia. The numbers are significant, the need is real, and the products that exist either misunderstand the users or ignore them entirely.

Curmunchkins starts from the other direction: design the experience for the kids who need it most, and it works better for everyone.

---

## What Curmunchkins Is

An edtech platform designed sensory-first for neurodivergent kids — specifically targeting ADHD, autism spectrum, dyslexia, dyspraxia, and related profiles.

The distinction between Curmunchkins and what exists in the market isn't feature depth. It's foundational philosophy.

**Sensory-first design** means the starting question isn't "what content do we teach?" It's "what environment makes learning possible for this user?" Sensory inputs — visual noise, auditory stimulation, pacing, transition speed, information density — are design variables, not afterthoughts. They're treated with the same intentionality as typography or color.

The hypothesis: if you get the environment right for a kid with sensory processing differences, the content has a chance. If you get it wrong, the content doesn't matter.

---

## The Design Problem

Designing for neurodivergent kids requires holding tensions that most design frameworks don't account for:

**Engagement vs. overwhelm.** The features that make products "sticky" for neurotypical users — notifications, animations, social feeds, infinite scroll — can be genuinely harmful for sensory-sensitive users. Curmunchkins has to be engaging enough to compete with everything else on a child's screen, but designed in a way that doesn't exploit attention or trigger overload.

**Structure vs. flexibility.** Kids with ADHD often benefit from clear structure and predictable patterns. Kids with autism spectrum profiles may need the same. But rigid, inflexible interfaces create their own anxiety. The design has to be predictable without being rigid — structured enough to reduce cognitive load, flexible enough to accommodate different working styles.

**Accessibility that doesn't signal accommodation.** Kids are acutely aware of being treated differently. A product that announces itself as "for kids with learning differences" creates its own barriers. The design has to be genuinely inclusive — not a separate lane for different learners, but a single experience that works well for the full range of users.

**Parent trust + child delight.** Curmunchkins has two users with different motivations. Parents need confidence: evidence that the product is good for their child, that it understands their child's specific profile, that it's safe. Children need delight: something that feels good to use, that doesn't feel like medicine. Getting both requires separate design thinking that has to cohere.

## The Founder / Designer Role

Curmunchkins is a founding role. That means carrying product strategy, design, brand, and user research simultaneously — without the separation that larger teams allow.

This requires a different kind of discipline: knowing when to be a founder (hold the vision, make resource decisions, navigate the market) and when to be a designer (get out of your own head, listen to users, build from evidence).

The tension is real. Founders have strong convictions about their product. Designers follow the data. The best founding designers learn to do both — to hold their convictions loosely enough that user feedback can change them.

The research work for Curmunchkins has required direct engagement with neurodivergent kids, their parents, and the educators who work with them. That work doesn't just inform features. It informs the entire frame of what the product is for.

## What's Different in Practice

Sensory-first design changes specific decisions:

**Motion is a lever, not a given.** Every animation in Curmunchkins is interrogated: does this serve the user, or does it serve the product's visual appeal? Transitions are slower, more predictable, more dismissible. The goal is never surprise.

**Typography choices carry therapeutic weight.** Font selection, size, line height, and letter spacing aren't aesthetic decisions in Curmunchkins — they're functional ones. Dyslexia-friendly typography is the default, not an option.

**Information density is a design variable.** The number of elements on screen at any given moment is a choice. Curmunchkins defaults to less. Whitespace isn't luxury design; it's cognitive load management.

**Pacing is user-controlled.** Kids with different processing speeds need different amounts of time. The product can't set a single pace and expect it to work. This affects content delivery, transition design, and session structure.

**Color is functional.** High contrast where it aids legibility. Reduced contrast where it aids calm. Color isn't used arbitrarily.

## Where It Is Now

Curmunchkins is in active development — current-phase product design, brand identity, and user research with target users and parents.

The longer arc is clear: the market is large, the incumbents are insufficiently thoughtful, and the timing is right. AI tools are creating new possibilities for personalized learning environments. The combination of sensory-first design philosophy and AI-powered personalization is the horizon the product is building toward.

## Why This Case Study Belongs in the Portfolio

Most portfolio pieces are about what you built. This one is about what you're building — and why.

The founding story of Curmunchkins is the clearest expression of the "Design Futurist" thesis: showing up to a problem before the map exists, with a point of view that the industry hasn't caught up to yet.

Neurodivergent-first design is not a niche. It's a better design practice. The industry will eventually figure this out. Curmunchkins is built on that premise, now.
