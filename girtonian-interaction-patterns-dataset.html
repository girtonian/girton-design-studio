02 / Storytelling Systems

How the Best Portfolios
Tell Stories

The portfolios getting noticed in 2026 are not resumes with nice typography. They're authored experiences with narrative logic, emotional arcs, and designed closure.

Key Insight from Research

Roman Jean-Elie's WebGL portfolio (Codrops Nov 2025) uses a portal that opens at page load and transforms into the word "MEET ME" on the contact page. His about section is divided into Cinema, Theater, and Code — not "Experience" and "Skills." That's the difference. Narrative architecture beats layout architecture every time.

Opening Act — First 3 seconds

The Hook: Something happens before you scroll

Video voxelization, origami unfold, or a particle field that assembles from noise into your name. The visitor must feel something before they read anything.

Rising Action — Scroll begins

The World: Each section is a room, not a row

On-scroll 3D layout formations, blurry text revealing as you descend, scroll-pinned elements that wait for you. The page reveals itself — you don't just scroll past it.

Climax — Work section

The Proof: Case studies that demonstrate, not describe

Cursor-as-preview-window, scroll-reactive mood galleries, inline video projection mapping demos. Show the Fjord installations in a WebGL recreation, not a screenshot.

Resolution — Contact

The Invitation: Narrative closure, not a form

A portal that transforms into text, a morphing shape that becomes "Let's build something." The ending is designed, not defaulted.

Girtonian Opportunity

Your background in projection mapping, immersive installations (Fjord/Accenture), gaming (Impact Theory), and design systems makes you the rare designer who can actually build these experiences — not just design them. That's your story. The website should be that proof-in-motion.

04 / Marketing as a Design Engineer

What Gets You Noticed
in the Agentic AI Era

Based on research across Codrops, Lovart, Supernova, CIO, The New Stack, and the design community in 2026. The window for differentiation is open — but narrowing.

The 2026 Hiring Reality

Over 93% of engineering leaders expect AI to transform productivity. Only 3% report transformational impact. The gap is filled by people who can redesign workflows, not just use tools. That is Jonathan Girton's exact brief. Own it explicitly.

01

Reframe: Creative Director of AI Agents

Stop describing yourself as a "Senior Product Designer using AI." Own the title: Design Engineer who orchestrates multi-agent creative workflows. In 2026, the threat isn't AI replacing designers — it's designers who manage 50 AI agents replacing those who don't.

  • Your Prompt Shop on Contra is proof you're already selling agent orchestration, not just design deliverables
  • Reframe every case study: "I directed AI agents to produce X while maintaining brand DNA"
  • Make your Make.com + Claude workflows visible on the site — they're the product
02

Build One Signature Demonstration

The most-hired creative developers have one thing: a single, unforgettable demo that circulates the community. Bilal Elmossaoui's scroll-driven 3D narrative. Sébastien Lempens' Paris scooter ride. What's yours?

  • Candidate A: A WebGL voxelization of your own face/avatar, tied to your Kyzen NFT work
  • Candidate B: A Fjord installation recreation — projection mapping in the browser
  • Candidate C: An agentic design demo — type a brief, watch Claude generate a design system in real time on-screen
  • Post on Codrops Creative Hub — they have 1,000+ demos and actively feature community work
03

Make Your Design System Machine-Readable

In 2026, design systems that can't be read by AI agents are becoming obsolete. Your United Airlines work is a proof point — document it as an MCP-compatible, AI-queryable system to demonstrate you understand where design systems are going.

  • Convert your UA Design System walkthrough into a structured JSON/MCP schema
  • Write a short article: "What I'd build differently about the UA design system for the agentic era"
  • Add an "AI-readable design system" service offering to your Girtonian services page
04

Storytelling as Proof, Not Decoration

Your biggest competitive advantage is that you've built experiences for 20,000 attendees (Fjord), 50,000 users (Kyzen), and global enterprise teams. Quantified storytelling — not features, but emotional outcomes with numbers attached — is what separates your portfolio from the market.

  • Rewrite every project summary: "28% DAU increase" → "28% more people returned the next day"
  • Lead with the human problem, end with the system you built to solve it
  • Use scroll-highlighted copy to call out the impact numbers as the reader passes over them
05

Contribute to Codrops / Creative Hub

Codrops published 200+ articles in 2025 and is actively building community. The Creative Hub has 1,000+ demos. A single accepted demo puts your work in front of the exact community that hires or recommends creative technologists.

  • Build one of the upgrade patterns above, then submit it as a Codrops tutorial
  • Your TCSEBO framework (from Prompt Shop strategy) would make an excellent interactive web demo/article
  • Document your AI design workflow as a tutorial — "Building a Design System with Claude as Copilot"
06

Differentiate: The Immersive Installation Angle

Almost no web portfolio designers can say they've built projection-mapped installations viewed by 20,000 people. This is a superpower in an era where physical-digital hybrid experiences are coming back via AR/MR. Lean into it hard.

  • Build an interactive video projection mapping demo using Three.js (Codrops Aug 2025 tutorial)
  • Frame Fjord work under "Spatial Design" not just "Installations" — it sounds futuristic, because it was
  • Connect it to the agentic AI theme: "AI-directed spatial experiences" is a defensible niche
