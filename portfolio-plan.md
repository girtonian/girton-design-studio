# Portfolio Plan: Designer × Creative Technologist × Product Strategist

---

## 1. Your Positioning Statement

Before anything else, your portfolio needs a **clear point of view**. A Designer/Creative Technologist/Product Strategist is rare — you sit at the intersection of *how things look*, *how they work*, and *why they should exist*.

Your positioning could be something like:

> "I design systems, build prototypes, and shape strategy — from the first sketch to the shipped product."

Or if you want to lean bolder:

> "I make things beautiful, functional, and worth building."

This statement should live front-and-center on your homepage — big, confident, and specific. It sets the tone before anything else.

---

## 2. Site Structure (Recommended Sections)

### `/ Home`
- **Hero:** Bold name + positioning statement. Not a job title — a *claim*. Think "I turn ambiguous problems into products people love" not "Product Designer at XYZ."
- **Highlight reel:** 3–4 project thumbnails that show range (visual, technical, strategic)
- **Short bio blurb** with a CTA: "View work" or "Let's talk"

### `/work` — Case Studies
Your main section. See section 3 for what to feature.

### `/about`
- Expanded bio — your story arc, not a resume
- What you believe in (design principles, philosophy, hot takes)
- Skills / tools (keep this visual, not a boring list)
- A photo (optional but humanizing — especially for the "warm collaborator" read)

### `/thinking` (optional but powerful)
- Essays, notes, or provocations — even 3–5 short ones
- This signals *how you think*, which is gold for founders and investors
- Could be a simple blog or a "notes" page

### `/contact`
- Keep it minimal. Email + LinkedIn + any relevant social (GitHub, etc.)
- Optional: a simple contact form

## 3. What to Feature: Work Strategy

Since you don't have case studies written yet, here's a framework for deciding what to include.

### The Portfolio Trifecta
A great Designer/Technologist/Strategist portfolio needs at least one of each:

| Type | What It Shows | Examples to Think Of |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Visual/Design piece** | Craft, aesthetics, taste | A brand identity, UI design, design system, poster/campaign |
| **Technical/Built piece** | You can actually make things | A prototype, app, tool, Figma plugin, script, automation |
| **Strategic/Thinking piece** | You understand the "why" | A product teardown, a pitch, a research synthesis, a product you helped define |

Aim for **3–5 projects total**. Quality over quantity — 3 brilliant ones beats 8 mediocre ones.

### How to Excavate Your Work
Even without "finished" case studies, think through:

1. **What problem did you help solve, define, or reframe?** (even if at a hackathon or side project)
2. **What have you built, shipped, or prototyped?** (doesn't need to be at a company — personal projects count)
3. **What did you design that you're proud of visually?** (an app screen, a brand, a system)
4. **What's a situation where your thinking changed the direction of something?**

### Project Frame (for each case study)
Use this structure to write up each one:

- **Problem:** What needed to exist or change, and why?
- **My role:** What were you specifically responsible for?
- **Process:** Key decisions, pivots, explorations (show the thinking)
- **Outcome:** What shipped, what changed, what was learned?
- **Visuals:** Mockups, flows, screenshots, sketches — the more the better

## 4. Tone & Design Direction

**Bold & expressive** for a Designer/Strategist says: *I have taste and I'm not afraid to use it.*

Concrete directions to consider:

- **Typography-led:** Big, editorial headlines. Mix a serif with a grotesque. Think magazine-meets-product.
- **High contrast:** Don't be afraid of black and white with one strong accent color
- **Motion as texture:** Subtle hover effects, scroll-triggered reveals — not flashy, but considered
- **Case study pages that breathe:** Give work room to shine. Don't cram everything in.

**Visual references to explore (for inspiration):**
- Linear.app (clean but confident)
- Lusion.co (bold creative studio energy)
- Personal sites of designers like Rauno Freiberg or Paco Courteille

---

## 5. Content Priorities (What to Write First)

Tackle in this order:

1. **Positioning statement** — your one-liner
2. **One strong case study** — pick the work you're most proud of and write it up first
3. **Bio / About** — your story and beliefs
4. **2–3 more case studies** — build the roster
5. **Home page copy** — once you know what's in the portfolio, the home page writes itself

---

## 6. Audience-Specific Signals

Since your audience is mixed, make sure each group can find what they need:

- **Recruiters & hiring managers:** Clear role labels, outcomes with context ("shipped to 10k users"), recognizable companies or institutions if applicable
- **Startup founders / clients:** Show scrappiness + taste + strategic thinking. Emphasize breadth and problem-solving speed.
- **Investors & collaborators:** Surface your *point of view* on products, markets, or design. Your /thinking section is key here.
